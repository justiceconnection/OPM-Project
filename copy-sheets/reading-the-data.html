3Data originreading-the-data:source.title
4All data is from the U.S. Office of Personnel Management's Federal Workforce Data. OPM publishes two types of monthly files: a snapshot of payroll demographics at the end of each month, and a record of personnel actions, including hires and departures.reading-the-data:source.p1
5Dashboard data begins in October 2011 and goes through present, with about a one month lag. Justice Connection updates the dashboard when OPM releases new data.reading-the-data:source.p3
6The Justice Department's components are shown as OPM reports them. DOJ consists of 30 component organizations, including litigating divisons, law enforcement agencies and U.S. Attorneys' Offices. The Community Relations Service was eliminated in April 2026 and OPM's files show no CRS employees after that month, so it is shown with 0 employees, hires and departures from May 2026. Note that CRS data is missing from OPM's January 2026 file, so that month shows 0 employees, but that is not an accurate count.reading-the-data:source.p4
7Each record is one person, but OPM's files have no personal identifier, so the dashboard cannot follow an individual from month to month.reading-the-data:source.p5
8Data breakdownreading-the-data:counting.title
9Employees are the number of staff counted at the month-end snapshot. For a quarter or a year, we show the count at the end of its last month, never an average or a sum.reading-the-data:counting.p1
10Hires and departures: counted in the month the action took effect, not the month OPM processed it. Most actions appear in the file for the month they took effect; since 2020, 98.7% appear within one month.reading-the-data:counting.p2
12Provisional labels are applied because some data is filed retroactively. The most recent three months are the most likely to change, and are largely marked provisional.reading-the-data:counting.p3
13Years are organized from October to September following the federal fiscal year. A year or quarter that is still in progress is marked partial and covers only the months published so far.reading-the-data:counting.p4
14Change in employees and hires minus departures are counted from different files, so they do not always match. The dashboard shows both and does not adjust one to fit the other. In most years they differ by less than 500.reading-the-data:counting.p5
15Rates and categoriesreading-the-data:rates.title
16A departure rate is the number of people who left, divided by the average number of employees over the same months. The average is taken over month-end counts. Quit and retirement rates work the same way, counting only those reasons.reading-the-data:rates.p1
17Hire rate includes all hires, new hires and transfers in, per 100 employees on average.reading-the-data:rates.hire
18You can choose how the rate is measured:reading-the-data:rates.p2
19Last 12 months: departures in the 12 months up to each point, over the average employee count in those months.reading-the-data:rates.list.a
20Fiscal year: departures in each fiscal year, over its average employee count. A year still in progress shows the year so far.reading-the-data:rates.list.b
21Annual pace: departures in each period, scaled up to a full year, over the period's average employee count.reading-the-data:rates.list.c
22Small sample: when a rate is based on fewer than 30 employees on average, it is shown with a flag. Such rates can swing widely from one period to the next. They are shown but caveated.reading-the-data:rates.p3
23While the human reasons behind a departure may be nuanced, in the data a departure falls into only one reason, based on OPM's separation codes:reading-the-data:rates.p4
24Individual and mass transfers to another agencyreading-the-data:rates.reasons.sep_transfer_out
25Voluntary, early and other retirementsreading-the-data:rates.reasons.sep_retirement
26Reduction in forcereading-the-data:rates.reasons.sep_rif
27Deferred Resignation Program (DRP): OPM flags departures under the program from March 2025. In the departure reasons charts they are shown as their own reason and are not counted again under Quit, Retirement or the other reasons.reading-the-data:rates.p5
28Hires can be either new hires (competitive, excepted or Senior Executive Service appointments) or transfers in from another agency.reading-the-data:rates.p6
29Departed staff demographics groups people by years of service, age (OPM's brackets), supervisory role and occupation. Groups are shown only by fiscal year or for 12-month periods, never month by month, so that small samples do not point to individuals. People whose group is unknown are counted in totals but not shown as part of a group.reading-the-data:rates.p7
30Known gaps and data issuesreading-the-data:gaps.title
31Gaps in the Deferred Resignation Program (affects fiscal years 2025 and 2026)reading-the-data:gaps.drp.title
32About 4,800 departures took effect in September 2025, most of them under the Deferred Resignation Program, but the employee count fell mostly in October 2025 at the end of the fiscal quarter (by 3,860). As a result, change in employees and hires minus departures differ by about 4,000 in each of fiscal years 2025 and 2026, in opposite directions. Across the two years the difference largely cancels out.reading-the-data:gaps.drp.p1
33Length of service recorded from 1900reading-the-data:gaps.los.title
34In OPM's departure files for June 2024 through July 2025, some records show 125 years of service, or the time since 1 January 1900 rather than since the person's start date. This affects 39 Justice Department staff who departed between October 2024 and March 2025. We treat those values as unknown: they are left out of the dashboard showing years of experience lost. The Look-Up tool shows OPM's values as published.reading-the-data:gaps.los.p1
35Occupational category missingreading-the-data:gaps.occ.title
36From June 2024, OPM's departure files no longer provide data in the broad occupational category. We group occupations by OPM's job series instead (examples include attorneys / 0905, criminal investigators / 1811, correctional officers / 0007).reading-the-data:gaps.occ.p1
37Pay and location withheldreading-the-data:gaps.redact.title
38OPM withholds pay and duty location for many employees--about two thirds of Justice Department records with most affecting the FBI, DEA, U.S. Marshals Service, ATF and the U.S. Attorneys' offices. The dashboard therefore does not show pay or location breakdowns.reading-the-data:gaps.redact.p1
39Revised filesreading-the-data:gaps.revisions.title
40OPM sometimes reuploads and changes a month's files. When that happens, the dashboard reloads them, and files that have been changed are flagged. Published numbers can change as a result.reading-the-data:gaps.revisions.p1
