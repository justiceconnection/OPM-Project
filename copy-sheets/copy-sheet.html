1Reading the datareading-the-data:page.title
2Where the data comes from, how it is counted, and what to keep in mind when reading it. Figures on this page are as of the August 2026 data.reading-the-data:page.intro
3Source and coveragereading-the-data:source.title
4All figures come from the U.S. Office of Personnel Management's Federal Workforce Data. OPM publishes two types of monthly files: a snapshot of everyone on the payroll at the end of each month, and a record of personnel actions, including hires and departures.reading-the-data:source.p1
5Dashboard data begins in October 2011 and goes through the latest month OPM has published. Justice Connection updates the dashboard when OPM releases new data, which usually lags by about a month.reading-the-data:source.p2
6"Justice Department" means every component of the Department.reading-the-data:source.p3
7The Department's components are shown as OPM reports them. The Community Relations Service was eliminated in April 2026; OPM's files show no CRS employees after that month, so it is shown with 0 employees, hires and departures from May 2026. It is also missing from OPM's January 2026 file, so that month shows 0 employees.reading-the-data:source.p4
8Each record is one person, but OPM's files have no personal identifier, so the dashboard cannot follow an individual from month to month.reading-the-data:source.p5
9Data breakdownreading-the-data:counting.title
10Employees: the number of people in the month-end snapshot. For a quarter or a year, we show the count at the end of its last month, never an average or a sum.reading-the-data:counting.p1
11Hires and departures: counted in the month the action took effect, not the month OPM processed it. Most actions appear in the file for the month they took effect; since 2020, 98.7% appear within one month.reading-the-data:counting.p2
12Provisional: because some actions arrive late, the newest three months can still change and are marked as provisional.reading-the-data:counting.p3
13Years are organized from October to September following the federal fiscal year. A year or quarter that is still in progress is marked partial and covers only the months published so far.reading-the-data:counting.p4
14Change in employees and hires minus departures are counted from different files, so they do not always match. We show both and do not adjust one to fit the other. In most years they differ by less than 500.reading-the-data:counting.p5
15Rates and categoriesreading-the-data:rates.title
16A departure rate is the number of people who left, divided by the average number of employees over the same months. The average is taken over month-end counts. Quit and retirement rates work the same way, counting only those reasons.reading-the-data:rates.p1
17Hire rate: all hires, new hires and transfers in, per 100 employees on average, counted the same way as the departure rate.reading-the-data:rates.hire
18You can choose how the rate is measured:reading-the-data:rates.p2
19Last 12 months: departures in the 12 months up to each point, over the average employee count in those months.reading-the-data:rates.list.a
20Fiscal year: departures in each fiscal year, over its average employee count. A year still in progress shows the year so far.reading-the-data:rates.list.b
21Annual pace: departures in each period, scaled up to a full year, over the period's average employee count.reading-the-data:rates.list.c
22Small groups: when a rate is based on fewer than 30 employees on average, it is shown with a flag. Such rates can swing widely from one period to the next; they are shown, not hidden.reading-the-data:rates.p3
23Every departure falls into exactly one reason, based on OPM's separation codes:reading-the-data:rates.p4
24individual and mass transfers to another agencyreading-the-data:rates.reasons.sep_transfer_out
25voluntary, early and other retirementsreading-the-data:rates.reasons.sep_retirement
26reduction in forcereading-the-data:rates.reasons.sep_rif
27Deferred Resignation Program (DRP): OPM flags departures under the program from March 2025. In the reasons charts they are shown as their own reason and are not counted again under Quit, Retirement or the other reasons; the tiles and rate lines still count them under their original reason.reading-the-data:rates.p5
28Hires are either new hires (competitive, excepted or Senior Executive Service appointments) or transfers in from another agency.reading-the-data:rates.p6
29Who is leaving groups people by years of service, age (OPM's brackets), supervisory role and occupation. Groups are shown only by fiscal year or for 12-month periods, never month by month, so that small groups do not point to individuals. People whose group is unknown are counted in totals but not shown as a group.reading-the-data:rates.p7
30Known gaps and data issuesreading-the-data:gaps.title
31The Deferred Resignation Program gap (fiscal years 2025 and 2026)reading-the-data:gaps.drp.title
32About 4,800 departures took effect in September 2025, most of them under the Deferred Resignation Program, but the employee count fell mostly in October 2025 (by 3,860). As a result, change in employees and hires minus departures differ by about 4,000 in each of fiscal years 2025 and 2026, in opposite directions. Across the two years the difference largely cancels out.reading-the-data:gaps.drp.p1
33Length of service recorded from 1900reading-the-data:gaps.los.title
34In OPM's departure files for June 2024 through July 2025, some records show about 125 years of service: the time since 1 January 1900 rather than since the person's start date. This affects 39 Justice Department departures between October 2024 and March 2025. We treat those values as unknown: they are left out of years of experience lost, and the coverage shown next to that figure reflects it. The Look-Up shows OPM's values as published.reading-the-data:gaps.los.p1
35Occupational category missingreading-the-data:gaps.occ.title
36From June 2024, OPM's departure files no longer fill in the broad occupational category. We group occupations by OPM's job series instead (attorneys 0905, criminal investigators 1811, correctional officers 0007).reading-the-data:gaps.occ.p1
37Pay and location withheldreading-the-data:gaps.redact.title
38OPM withholds pay and duty location for many employees: about two thirds of Justice Department records, and 78% to 90% at the FBI, DEA, U.S. Marshals Service, ATF and the U.S. Attorneys' offices. The dashboard therefore does not show pay or location breakdowns.reading-the-data:gaps.redact.p1
39Revised filesreading-the-data:gaps.revisions.title
40OPM sometimes reissues a month's files. When that happens we reload them, and months whose files changed are flagged. Figures on the dashboard can change as a result.reading-the-data:gaps.revisions.p1
